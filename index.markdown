# Part A
Southern New Hampshire University's Computer Science program has provided me with a broad foundation in computer science and has helped me better understand the skills and knowledge required to work in the technology field. Even though my intended career path is in data analytics, the program has given me exposure to many areas of development that will complement my analytical pursuits and make me a more versatile technology professional. Because of my coursework, I have developed foundational experience with Python, Java, and C++, while also gaining an understanding of object-oriented programming (OOP), create, read, update, and delete (CRUD) operations, software development methodologies, databases, software engineering, and security.

Learning several programming languages exposed me to different approaches to solving problems and has increased my versatility to adapt to what future employer require. My coursework in object-oriented programming also taught me how to organize programs around reusable objects and components, making my codework more modular. Similarly, learning CRUD operations gave me an understanding of how applications interact with and manipulate persistent data. These skills are particularly relevant to my goal of working in data analytics because effective analysis often depends on understanding how data is generated, stored, accessed, and transformed before it can be analyzed.

The program also introduced me to the larger development lifecycle. I was introduced to both Agile and Waterfall development methodologies and learned how different members of a development team contribute to a project. These roles included the Scrum Master, Product Owner, Developer, and Tester. Understanding these responsibilities helped me recognize that successful software development is not simply a matter of writing code. Development requires communication between individuals with different responsibilities and perspectives, as well as an understanding of the stakeholder's needs and project requirements.

My coursework also provided me with a foundation in data structures and algorithms. I gained experience with concepts such as arrays, binary search trees, and hash tables and developed an understanding of how different structures can be used to organize and retrieve information. Because these structures have different strengths and weaknesses, this has helped me recognize that selecting an appropriate way to organize data can affect the efficiency and effectiveness of a program. This knowledge is particularly valuable as I pursue data analytics because working with data requires an understanding of how information is structured and manipulated.

In software engineering, I developed an understanding of client-server architecture and the interactions between different components of an application. I also gained exposure to mobile application architecture and the ways to design an interface that is appealing to the end-user. These concepts broadened my understanding of software beyond individual programs and helped me see applications as interconnected systems designed with usablilty as the end goal. This systems-level perspective will be useful in my future career because data analysts frequently work with information generated by applications, databases, APIs, and other interconnected systems.

My studies in databases provided another important foundation for my professional goals. I learned about both SQL and NoSQL databases and gained an understanding of the different approaches they use to store and organize information. This knowledge is directly applicable to data analytics, understanding how data can be stored, queried, and manipulated is foundational knowledge for any Data Analyst. Learning about databases also reinforced the importance of data integrity, organization, and accessibility when developing technology solutions.

Finally, my coursework introduced me to the fundamental principles of computer security, including cryptographic hashing. Although security was not the primary focus of my intended career path, learning these concepts helped me understand the importance of protecting information and designing systems with security in mind. Data analytics frequently involves working with sensitive or valuable information, making an understanding of basic security principles an important part of becoming a responsible technology professional.

# Part B
In this capstone course of the Computer Science program we were asked to choose programming artifacts that we could enhance in order to showcase our knowledge in three categories "Software Engineering and Design, Algorithms and Data Structures, and Databases". As well has five course outcomes: "1. Employ strategies for building collaborative environments that enable diverse audiences to support organizational decision-making in the field of computer science. 2. Design, develop, and deliver professional-quality oral, written, and visual communications that are coherent, technically sound, and appropriately adapted to specific audiences and contexts. 3.Design and evaluate computing solutions that solve a given problem using algorithmic principles and computer science practices and standards appropriate to its solution while managing the trade-offs involved in design choices. 4. Demonstrate an ability to use well-founded and innovative techniques, skills, and tools in computing practices for the purpose of implementing computer solutions that deliver value and accomplish industry-specific goals. 5. Develop a security mindset that anticipates adversarial exploits in software architecture and designs to expose potential vulnerabilities, mitigate design flaws, and ensure privacy and enhanced security of data and resources.

I decided to choose a single artifact in which my enanhcements would show my proficiency in all categories and course outcomes. This artifact was the travlr Full-Stack application. The travlr application was originally an assignment in a previous course, CS465, that required me to use M.E.A.N. full-stack development tools, MongoDB, Express.js, Angular, and Node.js, to complete the "trips" portion of the application. I chose to continue developing this application because by applying the same concepts used to complete the "trips" functionality to the remaining sections of the website: "rooms", "meals", "news", "contact", and "about us", I could effectively demonstrate the skills I learned throughout the Computer Science program in a realistic manner. In other words I took an inherited application and improved its functionality.
